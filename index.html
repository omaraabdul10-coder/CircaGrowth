When does a late meal matter?

CircaGrowth is an interactive ordinary-differential-equation model linking circadian rhythm, glucose, insulin, and growth-hormone dynamics to study how meal timing can alter the simulated nocturnal STH peak.

The research question

Can the timing of food intake interact with the insulin-sensitive window around the STH peak?

The model represents glucose, insulin, tissue insulin action, an insulin absorption compartment, and growth hormone as coupled dynamical variables.

G(t)glucose
I(t)active insulin
X(t)insulin action
H(t)growth hormone
01 · Mathematical model

A coupled dynamical system

The computational core is a five-state ODE system. Food intake is represented as Gaussian glucose input, while the revised insulin component uses a pharmacokinetic absorption–clearance compartment.

dG/dt = RG(t) − (p1 + X)G + p1Gb
dX/dt = −p2X + p3(I − Ib)
dA/dt = ka,ampRG(t) − kaA
dI/dt = kaA + nIb − nI
dH/dt = SHC(t) · KI/(KI+I) − kHH

State variables

G(t)Blood glucose concentration · mg/dL
X(t)Tissue-level insulin activation effect
A(t)Insulin absorption compartment
I(t)Active plasma insulin · µU/mL
H(t)Growth hormone / STH · ng/mL

Circadian drive

C(t) = max(0, cos(2π(t − tpeak)/1440))

Simulation convention: t = 0 corresponds to 08:00; the default STH peak is 02:00.

03 · Research process

The important part was not only getting a curve.

The V1 model failed a face-validity test. Debugging exposed two structural issues, leading to the V2 formulation used here.

01

Face-validity test

A meal close to the STH peak should suppress the simulated STH peak more than a meal far from it.

02

Root-cause analysis

The original setup had an inconsistent equilibrium and a threshold insulin rule that produced an underdamped oscillatory response.

03

Mathematical revision

The equilibrium was made internally consistent and insulin secretion was replaced by an absorption–clearance compartment.

A key methodological lesson: parameters cannot safely be transplanted between sub-models without checking the context, time scale, inputs, and whether a variable is measured or modeled.

What the validation means

The model produces the expected ordering: a meal positioned close to the modeled nocturnal STH peak produces a larger simulated suppression than an early meal. This is a model-behavior validation, not a clinical validation.

Important: the project is a computational proof-of-concept and should not be interpreted as a medical recommendation.

05 · Limitations

What the model does not claim.

Keeping limitations visible makes the project more credible as a research artifact.

Single-person abstraction

Age, sex, body mass, insulin sensitivity, and individual variation are not represented.

Parameter context

Bergman minimal-model parameters came from an IVGTT context and are not fully adapted to meal-response data.

Empirical PK calibration

The insulin absorption parameter ka was empirically calibrated in this work rather than directly taken from one literature source.

Simplified STH rhythm

The circadian drive is a simple cosine with one modeled night peak rather than realistic multi-pulse ultradian secretion.

24-hour horizon

The current experiment covers one 24-hour cycle and does not model cumulative multi-day sleep disruption effects.

Not clinical validation

No conclusion about individual health outcomes should be drawn from this simulation alone.

06 · Why does this matter?

From a mathematical model to a biological question.

CircaGrowth is designed to explore how the timing of food intake may interact with the physiological dynamics surrounding nocturnal growth-hormone secretion.

Child & adolescent growth

Growth hormone is one component of the biological system involved in childhood and adolescent growth. The model provides a way to study, computationally, how meal timing could interact with the modeled STH signal during a nocturnal peak.

Muscle & tissue growth

Growth hormone participates in physiological processes associated with tissue growth and metabolism. CircaGrowth does not directly simulate muscle hypertrophy; instead, it models STH dynamics as a mechanistic intermediate that could be studied in future, more complete models.

Metabolic timing

Glucose and insulin dynamics are included because the model treats insulin as a time-dependent suppressing input to the STH equation. This lets the simulation ask when the timing of a meal matters most within the modeled system.

Sleep & circadian biology

The model represents a circadian drive for STH secretion and places a modeled peak in the night. This creates a computational framework for exploring interactions between feeding time and the sleep-associated hormonal window.

Personalized modeling — future direction

A future version could vary parameters for insulin sensitivity, body size, age-related physiology, or other experimentally justified factors. The current project deliberately remains a simplified single-person abstraction.

Research, not medical advice

The purpose is hypothesis generation and computational experimentation. The simulation does not establish that changing dinner time will increase height, muscle mass, or any individual health outcome.

The central idea is not to claim that the simulation predicts human growth. It is to build a transparent mathematical system that lets a researcher ask: under a defined set of assumptions, how does meal timing propagate through glucose and insulin dynamics into the modeled STH signal?
07 · Computational architecture

From equations to executable science.

CircaGrowth is not only a biological narrative. The project turns a coupled mathematical model into a reproducible computational pipeline: define the state, evaluate the vector field, integrate the system, validate behavior, and expose the parameters through an interactive interface.

State-space formulation

The model is represented as y′ = f(t,y;θ), where y = [G, X, A, I, H]. This makes every simulated quantity explicit and separates the dynamical state from parameters and external inputs.

Numerical integration

The browser implementation uses classical fourth-order Runge–Kutta integration. A fixed Δt = 1 minute gives 1,440 integration steps across a 24-hour experiment.

Deterministic simulation

For identical parameters and meal schedules, the solver produces the same trajectory. This makes parameter sweeps, controlled comparisons, and debugging reproducible.

Complexity

For this five-state system, each RK4 step evaluates the vector field four times. For N time steps, the numerical work is O(N·d), with d = 5 state variables, apart from plotting overhead.

Separation of concerns

The implementation separates the model equations, numerical solver, chart transformation, controls, and visualization. That structure makes the scientific core independently inspectable.

Model → interface

The dashboard does not hard-code a single result. Moving a control changes the input conditions, reruns the solver, and recomputes the STH peak relative to a control simulation.

08 · Numerical methods

Why RK4?

The computational method is part of the research, not an implementation detail hidden behind a graph.

Classical RK4 update

k₁ = f(t, y)
k₂ = f(t + Δt/2, y + Δt·k₁/2)
k₃ = f(t + Δt/2, y + Δt·k₂/2)
k₄ = f(t + Δt, y + Δt·k₃)
y(t+Δt) = y(t) + Δt(k₁ + 2k₂ + 2k₃ + k₄)/6

Numerical reasoning

The original Python implementation used SciPy's adaptive RK45 solver with tight tolerances and a maximum step of 1 minute. The interactive browser version independently implements fixed-step RK4 at the same 1-minute scale.

A useful computational question is not “does the graph look right?” but “does the numerical solution remain stable and consistent when the solver and step size are changed?”

A future numerical-validation module could compare RK4 against adaptive RK45, perform step-size convergence tests, and report maximum trajectory differences.

09 · Experimental design

Turn one simulation into a computational experiment.

The current interface changes dinner time and the modeled STH peak. The same architecture can support systematic parameter sweeps rather than relying on one hand-picked scenario.

One-factor sweeps

Vary dinner time across the available window while keeping all other parameters fixed. Record the STH peak for every condition.

Two-dimensional maps

Sweep both dinner time and STH-peak timing to create a response surface showing where the model is most sensitive.

Robustness analysis

Perturb parameters such as ka, KI, and kH and quantify whether the qualitative conclusion survives plausible changes.

Control design

Every intervention scenario is compared with a matched no-dinner control using the same STH-peak timing.

Reproducible outputs

For each run, the model can store the input parameters, meal schedule, trajectory, peak value, and derived suppression metric.

Future extension

A next version could export parameter-sweep data as CSV/JSON and generate publication-ready response surfaces and uncertainty plots.

10 · Debugging as research

The failed model became part of the scientific result.

The V1→V2 transition is one of the strongest computational parts of the project because the model was tested against a biological face-validity expectation rather than accepted simply because it produced smooth curves.

Failure mode 1 · equilibrium consistency

The initial parameter combination did not satisfy the model's own equilibrium condition. The resulting trajectory drifted from the intended baseline, making scenario comparisons unreliable.

γ(Gb − h) = nIb

Resolution: recalculate the relevant parameter so the initial state is mathematically consistent with the equilibrium condition.

Failure mode 2 · oscillatory insulin response

A hard glucose threshold coupled to the insulin-action dynamics created an underdamped response in V1. Insulin could collapse after meals, producing the wrong qualitative effect on simulated STH.

Resolution: replace the threshold secretion mechanism with an absorption–clearance compartment A(t), producing a smoother meal-response dynamic.
